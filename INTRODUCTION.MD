# The Cartographer

The first steps into the other world, are always done by a Cartographer and their Patron - Peres Whir, Cartographer of Asylum.

## Contents

### Introduction
There is nothing more impressive than a good Cartographer strapped with gear heading into the unknown, preparing to map and turn the secret into a tool for expansion and cultivation.

Before there can be heroes, there must be a map. The Map will tell you the secrets of the world you breathe life into, and of the conflicts that lay below the known surface. 
Using principles of Animancy, this Cartographer was designed for World Builders. Use it, and discover its limitless potential. 

### Where to Start
Begin first by learning the Layering System. Each Layer is meant to answer a different question about the world. Start with meaning, relationship, and pressure. Then add shape, distance, surface, structure, detail, and atmosphere. This keeps the map from becoming a drawing too early, and lets the world teach you what it needs before you dress it in final detail.

**Layer 0** — Animancy. 
Using Animancy as a base layer is the secret to a world that feels more than just worth exploring, it keeps it alive when you are failing to care for it. Animancy assists in discovering weak story lines, forgotten places, and excessive bloat.
- Nodes
- Edges
- Atmosphere
- Temporal State

**Layer 1** — Spatial Placement. 
Rough authored positioning of Nodes leads to a simplified exploration system. Otherwise known as Point Crawlers, this is the path that most entities of your world travel via. Rarely exiting their known safe path, you can use this to represent distances and journey lengths, without initial worry about specific measurements.
- Direction
- Height
- Depth
- Travel Lengths

**Layer 2** — Boundaries. 
This is the first layer that starts looking like a map. Polygons on a plane. The city has an outline. The wards have borders. The walls have gates. This gives yourself and entities an understanding of the shapes of their home. This tells them when they are in a safe area, when they are in a work area, and when they are outside of their known spaces.

- Ward shapes
- Zone Edges 
- Walls


**Layer 3** — Topology. 
By deciding the shape of the surface of your world, you now have a guide for converting a standard location into a layered rising metropolis, or a cavern system dropping down into an underwater spring. This step also increases understanding of travel challenges, and the extended difficulty that the entities traveling on it may face. 
- Elevations
- Terrain Names
- Rivers
- Towns
- Roads


**Layer 4** — Streets and Paths. 
The authored connections now become visible routes on the terrain surface instead of abstract lines between Nodes. A path is no longer only the knowledge that two places are connected. It becomes a road along a ridge, a tunnel under a wall, a bridge over black water, or a secret descent into a forgotten chamber. This is the Layer where travel gains texture.
- Streets
- Roads
- Footpaths
- Gates
- Bridges
- Tunnels
- Secret Routes

**Layer 5** — Landmarks and Features. 
This Layer places the things people remember. Buildings, temples, taverns, monuments, ruins, wells, shrines, towers, markets, and lairs are given specific positions within the spaces that contain them. A district stops being only a named region and starts becoming a place with anchors. This is where the Map becomes navigable by memory, rumor, habit, and fear.
- Buildings
- Temples
- Taverns
- Monuments
- Ruins
- Markets
- Lairs

**Layer 6** — Texture and Detail. 
This Layer gives material character to what has already been authored. Terrain color, stonework, roof material, water quality, vegetation, smoke, mud, age, damage, and ornamentation all belong here. It should not replace the earlier Layers. It should reveal them. A wealthy ward may shine with polished brass and clean roads, while a wounded ward may sag under broken timber, sour water, and ash.
- Terrain Color
- Building Materials
- Water Quality
- Vegetation
- Damage
- Age
- Ornamentation

**Layer 7** — Atmosphere Rendering. 
This is where Animancy becomes visible as environmental presence. Fog, light, weather, time of day, sound, shadow, and motion express the pressure already discovered in Layer 0. Spiritus may become a steady breathing light. Fermentum may become flickering torchlight and haze. Pulsus may become moonlight that shifts with a rhythm. Silent may become still air, dead lamps, and color drained from the stones. This is the Layer where the place feels inhabited, even when no one is on screen.
- Fog
- Light
- Weather
- Time of Day
- Sound
- Shadow
- Motion

The Layers are not a strict prison. They are a method. If a later idea appears early, write it down, then decide which Layer should hold it. A good Cartographer does not force the world to arrive finished. They let it become legible one Layer at a time.
